The pitch

Outsource your music theory.

Most producers lose their creative momentum the second they have to figure out a chord progression. You've got a beat and a vibe, but then you're stuck hunting for notes and fighting inversions. The R1C1 makes asking "what if this was a lush Neo-Soul 13th chord?" a one-knob decision instead of a Wikipedia rabbit hole. Just pick a bank, press a key, and let the hardware do the math.

Welcome to R1C1.

A five-step orientation, per company policy.

1

Plug it in

Connect USB to your computer — Ableton, Logic, FL Studio, it just works — or run DIN OUT straight into a hardware synth. No drivers, no setup.

2

Open the spreadsheet

Single-click the encoder to step into the eight-cell screen. Twist to move between cells; click again to edit; click once more to step back.

3

Pick a bank & a root

Land on the BANK cell and choose your flavour — try RIVIERA TOUCH. Set ROOT to taste. The keys light up in the bank's colour.

4

Play the grid

Each key is a chord. Top rows are the lower octave; bottom rows climb higher. Hold two keys and the R1C1 invents a passing chord between them.

5

Push the slider

In its resting SPREAD mode, easing the slider up fattens the voicing live. That's the sound of the sun going down.

OK — let's play
Performance
How the grid is laid out

Playing chords.

The rows are scale degrees; the slider and encoder do the styling. Three things to know:

One key
A whole chord

Every key returns a full voicing from the active bank. Top rows sit low; bottom rows climb an octave higher, so one hand spans a wide range.

Two keys
A bridge chord

Hold two keys and the R1C1 generates the passing chord between them — a dominant or diminished pivot, auto voice-led into range.

Double-tap shift
Hold mode

Latch the chord so it keeps ringing while your hands are free — for the slider, the encoder, or a cold drink. SHIFT glows amber.

Voicing
Inversions & voice leading

It moves the notes for you.

Twist the encoder to roll the chord through inversions, from -3 to +3 — each step lifts or drops one note by an octave.

Twist the encoder to AVL (Auto Voice-Leading). Instead of a fixed inversion, the R1C1 becomes your invisible session player. It calculates the smartest, smoothest path from your last chord to your next one, gluing your progression together so perfectly that people will think you actually took piano lessons.

One ribbon, three jobs

The slider.

Hold SHIFT and move the slider to pick a mode; release to commit. The four LEDs always tell you where you are.

Spread

The resting mode. Push up to fatten the voicing live — lower octaves and upper extensions stack in as you climb.

Velocity

The slider becomes dynamics, sweeping from a whisper at 30 up to a slam at 127.

Arp

The slider sets the arpeggiator rate, from Off through 1/4 down to a blurring 1/32.

Special — Arp
The arpeggiator

Arp.

Move the slider into its top zone and your held chord starts to dance. Hold SHIFT and twist to change the pattern.

Browse the sounds.

Seven raw scales and fourteen voiced banks, filed by mood. Open a folder, tap a sound, and it loads straight onto the device.

New in Firmware 1.13

The R1C1 doesn't just transpose notes. Each bank has its own intelligent ruleset — dropping roots for funk, clamping registers for dub, or adding thick doublings for Italo disco.
